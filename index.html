About

I work in the research department at FlashLabs, where I co-lead Chroma, a real-time end-to-end spoken dialogue model that reasons over what it hears. My research asks how AI systems detect and recover from errors in the evidence they use.

In cascaded spoken question answering, speech becomes text, text guides retrieval, and retrieved evidence supports reasoning. An error at one stage can be amplified by later components, even when those components score better on clean input. My EMNLP 2026 paper measures that amplification in multi-hop RAG, and IndexRAG asks what belongs in the index rather than the query path.

Earlier work explored multi-agent reinforcement learning with graph neural networks, specifically generalization across unseen network topologies, and test-time adaptation for EEG foundation models.

  • Spoken dialogue. End-to-end models that hold a real-time conversation and reason over speech.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation. Architectures that pre-compute cross-document inference at index time.
  • Robustness. How errors introduced upstream by speech recognition are amplified rather than absorbed as a question travels through multi-hop retrieval.
